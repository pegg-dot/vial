VIAL · Product & UX audit · 21 Jul 2026

You built a Bloomberg Terminal.
Now make it talk like a person.

A section-by-section walk of every buyer-facing surface — who each is for, its one job, what's confusing, and whether to keep, merge, or cut it. The engine underneath is strong. The problem is that it introduces itself in architecture jargon and spreads one buyer across thirty screens.

The one-line verdict

There is one buyer and one job: "look up a peptide or a vendor, see who's actually legit, and buy without getting scammed." Everything that serves that job stays and gets rewritten in human language. Everything that serves the machine — the control plane, the registry, the developer API, the sandbox checkout — comes out of the buyer's path. That collapses ~13 nav items to 5 and ~31 surfaces to a tight spine of about 10.

Confirmed  We're going affiliate-out, not native checkout. That single decision deletes the entire cart / checkout / orders branch — the most jargon-heavy, least buyer-shaped part of the app.

The north star

Who this is for, in one sentence

A fitness / weight-loss / longevity buyer — not a scientist — who is about to spend $50–$300 on a peptide and is scared of buying a fake. They don't want a "provenance control plane." They want the answer to three questions:

  • Is it real? — has this exact batch been independently lab-tested, and did it pass?
  • Will I get scammed? — is this vendor trustworthy, or a fresh domain with astroturfed reviews?
  • Where's the best price? — same compound, every vendor, side by side.

VIAL already computes all three. The audit below is about getting them to the surface and getting the machinery out of the way. The framing to hold the whole time: powerful terminal underneath, dead-simple on top, science tucked behind a "details" link for the 5% who want it.

Structural fix #1 — the nav

From a directory of the codebase to a shop you can walk

Today's top nav exposes the internal object model — For you, Market, Search, Compounds, Vendors, Evidence, Passports, Signals — plus an "Intelligence" footer column that lists Labs, Testing, Operations, Status. A buyer can't tell Evidence from Passports from Testing, because those are three views of one idea: proof.

Say it the way a buyer would

The pattern to fix everywhere: every page opens with an abstract noun the buyer never uses — "Normalize the market," "Persistent identity graph," "Provenance control plane," "Decision continuity." Lead instead with the buyer's stake. Here are the highest-leverage rewrites, top of funnel down.

After

After

The affiliate shift

What "affiliate-out, not checkout" actually removes

Confirming your call: VIAL is Yahoo Finance, not the brokerage. It shows the market and links you out. Concretely, that decision:

  • Deletes three buyer surfaces — /cart, /checkout, /account/orders — and everything they drag in (the "policy-gated checkout / underwriting" narrative the buyer never needed to read).
  • Turns one button into the whole business model — "Add to sandbox cart" becomes "Buy at [Vendor] →", an outbound affiliate link. That's the revenue line and it keeps VIAL's hands clean of money and product.
  • Protects neutrality — because VIAL earns on the click-out, not on ratings, the money layer stays separate from the truth layer. Guard this hard: a paying vendor must never be able to buy a better reputation record.
  • Removes a legal gray zone — no payments, no fulfillment, no "did VIAL sell me a research chemical" exposure.

One honest caveat to decide together: the sandbox-commerce engine (v5) is real, working code. Cutting it from the buyer path is the right call — but it's a chunk of the app going dormant. It stays in the repo; we just stop routing buyers through it.

What I'd do next — your call on each

Sequenced, and nothing touched yet

This is an audit, so I've changed zero code. The moment you're aligned on the map above, the execution order I'd suggest:

  • 1 · Copy pass. Land every rewrite above plus the rest of the surfaces. Pure text, low risk, instantly makes the product feel human. This is the fastest visible win.
  • 2 · Nav & merge. Collapse to the 5-door nav; build "How we check"; move machine surfaces out of the buyer path. Reversible, no data touched.
  • 3 · Affiliate swap. Replace cart/checkout with the outbound "Buy at vendor" link; retire the three commerce surfaces from nav.
  • 4 · Make the data real. Point the existing ingestion engine at Janoshik's public COA DB + ~3 real vendor sites for one compound end-to-end, as proof the pipeline works on live data.
  • 5 · Visual polish. The design pass — once the surfaces are the right ones and the words are right, so we polish the final thing, not a draft.

My recommendation: start with the copy pass on the home page and the vendor/product detail views — that's the buyer's actual path to a decision, and it's where the jargon does the most damage. Tell me which surfaces to start on and I'll execute.
